# Profitability assumptions for GEEZ AI STUDIO

This is a structural cost model, not a forecast or evidence of profit. The owner is considering a worldwide AI image/video service funded by monthly subscriptions, with a once-only free trial. No actual paying customers, paid conversion, retention, measured provider invoices or marketing acquisition costs have been established.

## Evidence and gaps

**Known:** draft monthly prices are $5/$10/$20/$50; generation is metered; the Free Preview includes 3 images and one six-second video. The owner's Replicate pricing screenshots show FLUX Schnell at $3 per thousand images, FLUX 1.1 Pro at $0.04/image, and two example video models at $0.09/$0.25 per second of output. These are model-specific advertised rates, not measured invoices, verified launch integrations, or proof that every model supports the trial duration. Check the selected model's current [official price](https://replicate.com/pricing), commercial terms and exact settings before activation.

**Missing:** approved payment fees and fixed per-charge fee, real model/settings mix, actual billed attempts and failed/uncertain work, storage/bandwidth, permanent hosting/database cost, refunds/chargebacks, free-user conversion, support time, acquisition cost, renewal retention, payout/FX fees, and tax treatment. These inputs prevent a reliable net-profit forecast.

## Formula

```
monthly operating profit before income tax and owner pay =
  subscription revenue excluding collected taxes
  - payment, payout and currency-conversion fees
  - all billed AI generation, including free trials and billable failures
  - hosting, database, storage and bandwidth
  - support and maintenance
  - marketing and acquisition
  - refunds, disputes and other operating costs
```

Monthly recurrence makes revenue more predictable only if users stay subscribed and usage remains affordable. An allowance must protect margins even when a customer uses it fully. Do not base a plan on customers forgetting to cancel or leaving credits unused. Cancellation must remain accessible.

## Sensitivity example, not actual results

For a hypothetical **$10/month** customer, assume $1 payment/payout fees and $1 allocated hosting/storage/support. These values are assumptions, not approved fees or measured expenses:

| Scenario | Assumed monthly AI cost | Remaining contribution before marketing, refunds, tax and owner pay |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Low usage | $2 | $6 |
| Middle usage | $4 | $4 |
| Heavy usage | $8 | $0 |

At 100 hypothetical middle-usage subscribers, this would be $1,000 revenue and $400 remaining before those other costs. It is not $400 proven profit. Growth can lose money if acquisition, churn, refunds or trial usage exceed the remaining contribution.

For a six-second video **only if a chosen model supports that duration**, the example screenshot rates imply $0.54 or $1.50 of video inference before processing, storage or retries. Three $0.003 images plus one such video would cost about $0.549 or $1.509 per fully used free trial before overhead. The advertised cheap image rate cannot be applied to premium video. A hundred completed trials could therefore consume about $54.90 or $150.90 before overhead at those example settings. This is why the real free video model, abuse protection, and a hard free-user budget must be verified before activation.

## Smallest validation plan

1. Confirm gateway product approval and bank payout eligibility before encouraging further funding.
2. Test a small, explicitly budgeted set of real image/video settings. Record actual billed cost, latency, failure/retry cost and acceptable output quality.
3. Reprice model credit quotes against full monthly allowance usage, processor fees and operating overhead. Keep model-specific prices, spending caps, and monthly limits.
4. Run a small invited beta. Measure paid conversion, trial cost per payer, monthly spend per active customer, cancellation/renewal, refunds and acquisition cost. Compare contribution per customer with acquisition and retention costs before increasing the trial budget or paid promotion.

This supports a possible profitable service; it does not establish demand or guarantee a profit.
